# Voice and tone

## Guidance

Our writing has two dimensions: voice and tone.

Our voice reflects who we are, it never changes. We should always sound like Talkdesk.

In contrast, some aspects of our personality might be more or less apparent, depending on the audience
and their context (think different users, like an admin vs a regular agent; empty state vs description
of a header; a recurrent message vs a rare one). That's tone.

---

## Voice

Talkdesk is a young, vibrant company that has taken the contact center industry by storm with its
innovative products and a new way of tackling perennial, quasi-unsolvable problems.

We should all be proud of what we've accomplished so far, but youth and hypergrowth have drawbacks: it's
difficult to deliver consistency and intuitiveness – cornerstones of any leading software. And when these
are lacking, trust becomes an issue.

So what can we do? We make sure our voice is as clear and predictable as water. Building trust is our top
priority, and that's why our writing is always **clear**, **positive**, **concise**, and **honest**. We
sound like a friendly and knowledgeable colleague, who uses everyday language to guide everyone through
all of our products.

---

## Tone

As the good, friendly and knowledgeable colleague that we are, there will come times when we'll show more
enthusiasm. We will be witty and irreverent, but never awkward or offensive. We'll know when the time is
right because we understand the context. The same joke told over and over again stops being funny very
quickly. We'll congratulate a user for creating something complex but not for something as simple as
closing a window. If we know the user is in a hurry, we'll get out of their way.

**If something serious has gone wrong**, we say "sorry". For instance, if the product has stopped working
completely.

However, we avoid saying "sorry" in other types of error messages. We also **avoid humor when writing for
errors**. People use our products for serious tasks and can sometimes be under stress.

There's usually no need to say "please" or "thank you". It's alright to say "Contact created" rather than
"Thank you for creating a contact".

Remember to focus on the specifics of the situation and adapt the tone accordingly.
