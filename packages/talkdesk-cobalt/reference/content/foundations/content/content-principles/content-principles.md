# Principles

## Guidance

### Be concise, simple, and direct

Avoid unnecessary and redundant information. Avoid large blocks of text and long, complex sentences.
This will help users to be more efficient in their work and prevent cognitive overload. By the way,
direct does not mean insensitive.

**Do:** "Save changes?"

**Don't:** "Would you like to save your changes?"

---

### Be honest and specific

Be honest and specific about what the product can or cannot do. Don't oversell or overpromise.

**Do:** "You can add up to 50 guests to a conversation."

**Don't:** "You can add as many guests as you want to a conversation."

---

### Use positive language

**Do:** To open Callbar, click Open.

**Don't:** You can't open Callbar if you don't click Open.

**Note:** Positive language doesn't mean you cannot use "negative" words. *You can disable Callbar
anytime* is a sentence that gives the user confidence despite having the word "disable" in it. It's more
about the message and how we make the users feel than the words individually.

---

### Use active voice

**Do:** "Please install the app first."

**Don't:** "The app must be installed first."

**Exception:** When you want to specifically emphasize the action over the subject. In some cases, this
is fine. E.g., *Your account has been flagged.*

---

### Write for all reading levels

Use technical and industry terminology when it's relevant but keep your language as simple as possible.

**Do:** "Preparing video…"

**Don't:** "Buffering..."

---

### Address users clearly

Use "you" and "your" to address your users or use a "No point of view" approach.

**Do:** "Please check your internet connection."

**Don't:** "I have to check my internet connection."

**Do:** "Account"

**Don't:** "My Account"

**Exception:** There are some cases in which it's industry standard to use "I" and "my" to emphasize the
user's ownership of content or actions.

**Do:** "I agree to follow the terms of service."

**Don't:** "You agree to follow the terms of service."

---

### Write in the present tense

Use the present tense to describe product behavior. Avoid using the future tense to describe the way a
product always acts. When you need to write in the past or future tenses, use simple verb forms.

**Do:** "Contact created"

**Don't:** "Contact has been created"

---

### Avoid slang and jargon

Use a conversational, casual tone with our users, but don't be overfamiliar or condescending.

**Do:** "Well done"

**Don't:** "You're the real deal, bro!"

---

### Don't exclude people

Never rely on shape, size, color or location alone to communicate something, because not all users will
know what you're talking about. Avoid saying things like:

* "Click the blue button."
* "Go to the menu on the top of the page."
* "Fill out your details in the square box."
