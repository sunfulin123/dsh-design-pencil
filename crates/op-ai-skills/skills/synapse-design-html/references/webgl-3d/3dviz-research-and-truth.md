# Research and truth

Research the claims the viewer will infer, not just domain keywords. A crystal heart may be decorative; a cutaway labeled with chambers makes anatomical claims. A fantasy agent village may use invented creatures but its task/tool/memory relationships still need grounding if it explains a real harness.

Start with appropriate sources: official definitions, standards, textbooks, university material, research papers or source datasets. Read the relevant passage; a search snippet is not a verified claim. Record the source locator, version or date where relevant, what it supports and its assumptions. Cross-check when ambiguity or impact warrants it, not to reach a source count.

Map claim → model → visual: units and frames, geometry/topology, state/equations, visual motion, labels and checks. A source about notation does not certify a solver. An output that compiles does not prove the source was modeled correctly.

If sources conflict, distinguish conventions, versions and regimes. If evidence is unavailable, narrow the claim or mark it unverified, and continue independent work. Ask only for information that actually blocks the user’s intended result.

A faithful citation can still repeat an error or an outdated teaching model. Resolve a consequential disagreement with another appropriate source and preserve the scope of the correction. Before adopting a proposed invariant, check a simple counterexample and reconcile it with the allowed actions: backward timeline replay, for example, is different from reversing the modeled physical process. Prefer a narrower accurate check to a confident but false universal rule.

Useful artifact notes, when needed:

- Claim and scope; source with locator; assumptions and simplifications.
- Elements conveying the claim and the check performed.
- Source status separately from artifact check status and observer.

Changing color does not require researching all established facts again. Changing labels, scale, motion semantics or geometry may change the claim and warrant a targeted re-check.
